# Central hypothesis — what the article corpus can and cannot observe

**Read-only. The hypothesis is not changed, restated or re-scoped by this document.**

The hypothesis under test: *AI does not inherently shrink or expand the services
market; it redistributes spend.* Buyer outcomes are BANKED, REDEPLOYED or
UPGRADED, and the provider proposition is that providers win by converting
efficiency into new demand.

`00-programme/contract-tracker/MEASURE-FIT.md` ruled on the public procurement
corpus: BANKED and REDEPLOYED NOT OBSERVABLE, UPGRADED and demand-creation
CORROBORATING ONLY. Those rulings stand for that source. This is the same
question asked of the article corpus.

Date: 2026-09-08 · evidence base: the frozen evaluation set (107 readable
articles, 54 contract events with a tracked provider).

| state | verdict from articles | why |
|---|---|---|
| **BANKED** — the client kept the saving | **NOT OBSERVABLE** | an article reports a provider's award. It almost never reports a client's budget, and never reports a budget that did not move. The absence of a later contract is not evidence of banking: it is equally consistent with work going to a provider off the roster, to a framework no feed covers, or in-house. The procurement corpus cannot see this and neither can the press |
| **REDEPLOYED** — the saving came back as new scope | **WEAKLY OBSERVABLE, single-case only** | this needs three things: a saving, a later engagement, and evidence linking them. Articles occasionally supply all three in one passage — a scope reduction with a stated value alongside redeployment of the same staff. One such case appears in the evaluation set (a provider losing $50m of scope with staff moved to other work at the same client). One case is an anecdote, not a rate. Route it as a named case study, never as a share |
| **UPGRADED** — the same money buys higher-value work | **CORROBORATING** | genuinely improved on the procurement corpus. Event type and AI relevance are now observed per event rather than inferred from a classifier, so a shift within one buyer–provider pair from NOT_AI_SPECIFIC run work to EXPLICIT_AI build work is visible where both events are reported. It corroborates; it does not measure, because the unreported half of the pair is invisible |
| **demand-creation proposition** | **CORROBORATING** | expansions of an existing relationship are observed as their own event type (6 of 54 events) and separable from new logos (33 of 54). That is the shape the proposition predicts. The *mechanism* — efficiency converted into new demand — is not stated by any source, and the trend is dominated by disclosure bias below |

## Disclosure bias — the measured limitation

The procurement corpus's bias is geographic and sectoral: Australia and the UK
are 82.7% of it, and every feed is public-sector. The article corpus has the
opposite bias, and it is just as strong.

- **Announcements outnumber outcomes.** 39 of 54 events are ANNOUNCED, 15
  COMPLETED. Awards are news; delivery is not.
- **Wins outnumber losses.** 33 NEW_WIN against 1 COMPETITIVE_TAKEAWAY and no
  terminations in the sample. Providers publish wins. Clients rarely publish
  the incumbent's name, and nobody publishes a quiet non-renewal.
- **Values are mostly withheld.** 13% of events state a value; 4 of 42
  private-sector events do. A value-weighted view of this corpus describes the
  subset that discloses, not the market.
- **Large and English-language deals dominate.** Coverage follows listed-company
  materiality thresholds and English-language press.
- **74% of stored articles carry no readable body text** (feed link markup or a
  headline). That is a collection defect, not a market fact, and it is
  quantified in the pipeline report. Until it is repaired, absence of an event
  is weak evidence of anything.

**Consequence for the hypothesis.** The corpus is a good instrument for *what
kind of work is being bought* and a poor one for *how much*. Redistribution of
spend is a magnitude claim. This corpus can show the shape of the shift and
supply named cases; it cannot size it, and no aggregate over these events should
be presented as market size or as a share of anything.

## What must not be inferred from this

- No hypothesis, question, weight or mapping is changed.
- No event here is promoted into an analytical value.
- Counts in this document describe a 107-article sample, not the corpus.
